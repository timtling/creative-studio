# Art sources and studio benchmarks

Two things this file is for: giving the studio somewhere to reach when the work is going polite, and setting the quality bar.

**How to use it.** Everything here is a **principle to take**, never a look to copy. A board cites the principle and links the source. Nothing here is named in a generation prompt, no project is imitated, and no territory is ever pitched as "like Studio X" — that is a description of somebody else's work standing in for an idea about this client.

The studios below are studied because they are bold and systemic at the same time. That combination is the bar: expressive work that still holds together as a system, rather than a choice between the two.

---

## Part one: art practice

Movements and practices, as principles.

### Depth and space

- **Constructivism** (Rodchenko, Stepanova, Lissitzky). Diagonal structure as the carrier of meaning: the composition is doing the arguing, not the words in it. Take the structural diagonal and the sense that a layout can have force; leave the red-and-black revolutionary costume, which is now a look.
- **Op art** (Riley, Vasarely). Depth made from flat marks alone, with no rendering, no shadow and no 3D. When a brand has no photography and no budget, this is where depth comes from.
- **Josef Albers, *Interaction of Color***. A colour is changed by what it sits next to, so a palette is a set of relationships rather than a set of values. Take the discipline of testing a colour in place rather than in a swatch.
- **Light and space** (Turrell, Irwin). Material that behaves differently as you move or as the light changes. The principle for an identity that has to work on a screen, a wall and a vehicle.

### Colour

- **Fauvism** (Matisse, Derain). Colour released from description: it does not have to be the colour the thing is. The permission structure for a palette that is not literal.
- **Sonia Delaunay**. Colour as simultaneous contrast across a whole applied system — textiles, cars, interiors — rather than as an accent on a page. The model for a palette designed to be *worn*, not just printed.
- **Mexican modernism** (Barragán). Saturated colour used architecturally, in large planes, with restraint in everything else. The answer to "how is this bold without being loud".

### Texture and material

- **Arte povera** (Merz, Pistoletto). Ordinary materials treated seriously. Take the principle that the material's own character is the content; useful when a brand is about work rather than polish.
- **Bauhaus weaving** (Anni Albers, Gunta Stölzl). Structure and pattern generated by a system with a fixed vocabulary. The direct ancestor of a good token system, and proof that a constraint produces variety rather than sameness.
- **Risograph and screen print**. Misregistration, limited inks, overprint. Take overprint as a way to get a third colour from two, and the honesty of a process that shows its own making.

### Shape and form

- **Suprematism** (Malevich). A shape can carry meaning with no representation at all. The reason a mark does not have to depict anything.
- **Memphis** (Sottsass). Rule-breaking as a system rather than as chaos — it has its own grammar. Take the grammar, not the squiggles, which are on the anti-generic list.
- **Concrete poetry**. Type as an object with a position, not only as text to read. The ancestor of a typographic system that behaves.
- **Brutalist architecture**. Form following construction honestly, at scale, with weight. Take the honesty and the weight, not the concrete texture.

## Part two: studio benchmarks

Five studios, looked at on 25 September 2026. Each principle names the project it came from, with its link. Open them before relying on the detail here.

### DIA — [dia.studio](https://www.dia.studio/)

Type, motion and system as one discipline. Its [index](https://www.dia.studio/index) is filed by *Identity, Tools, Type, Experiential, Campaigns, Film, Education*, which is itself a position: tools sit beside identity as deliverables of the same weight.

1. **Design the loop, not the frame.** [Nike Basketball](https://www.dia.studio/work/nike-basketball): "We fused the design and animation process into one, resulting in pieces with no end or beginning. These unique loops create a hypnotic impression that is impactful in both large and small formats." Take the fusing — motion decided at the same time as the form, not applied to a finished layout — and the test that it has to survive both scales.
2. **Let the process be the idea.** [Klim Söhne](https://www.dia.studio/work/klim-foundry): analogue films made with "printing techniques, chemicals, and in some cases fire", timing bent afterwards. The making carries the argument about the typeface. Where a brand is about material or craft, the production method can say it more convincingly than a layout can.
3. **A typeface can be an argument about history.** The same project frames Söhne as "the memory of Akzidenz-Grotesk framed through the reality of Helvetica". Type choice as a position rather than a preference — the level of reasoning the studio's own type decisions should reach.
4. **Ship tools, not just artefacts.** The Tools section (Scan Type, Mapping, Accordian) treats built instruments as legitimate output. When a client's problem repeats, the deliverable may be the thing that makes the next one, not another one.

### Studio Dumbar — [studiodumbar.com](https://studiodumbar.com/)

Public-sector rigour and motion, in the same studio.

1. **Restraint is a strategy, not a default.** [Dutch National Police](https://studiodumbar.com/work/dutch-police): "we focused on a sober, honest viewpoint, seeking to focus on character and strength, clarity and transparency." Sober because the subject demanded it and argued as such — which is what separates restraint from timidity.
2. **Design the applied system before the mark.** Same project: the angled stripes across cars, motorcycles, boats and helicopters were the identity, "voluntarily adopted by all of The Netherlands' emergency services". The thing people actually see is the applied system.
3. **Presence is a design outcome you can measure.** Same project: "Research revealed that the public thought the police had increased their presence on the streets, when in actual fact the number of cars had decreased." Worth holding up against any claim that a brand's effect cannot be measured.
4. **Motion as the identity, at festival scale.** [DEMO 2025](https://studiodumbar.com/work/demo-2025): a motion identity across 15 cities, artworks re-cut to each screen's dimensions, with sonic design "ethereal and spacious… alongside heavier and textural ones". Take the format-specific re-cut: one asset stretched to every screen is the cheap version.

### Hey Studio — [heystudio.es](https://heystudio.es/)

Geometry, flat colour and warmth, sustained across an unusually wide range of media.

1. **Build a system that generates variety from one part.** [Kaleos](https://heystudio.es/project/kaleos/): "a single frame that can be customized into 1296 different styles", explicitly so that variety does not require more objects. A constraint producing range rather than limiting it — the argument for a token system, made in a physical product.
2. **Let the system carry an ethic.** Same project: the one-frame approach "reduces environmental impact but also promotes smarter consumption". The structure of the system is the position, not a claim written next to it.
3. **Geometry plus a full palette is not childish.** Across the [work index](https://heystudio.es/work/) — Kaleos, Time For Fun, Cuptural, Origo — flat geometric construction and saturated colour carry adult categories. The counter-example to the belief that seriousness requires desaturation.
4. **Range is a studio capability.** The same index runs packaging, signage, editorial, product, motion and identity. A studio that can only make websites will eventually make every brand look like a website.

### ManvsMachine — [mvsm.com](https://mvsm.com/)

"A multi-dimensional creative company specialised in the fields of design, film and visual arts." Motion, CGI and material as the primary language rather than the finish.

**Read this entry with a caveat.** The individual case pages would not open for me; I read the [work index](https://mvsm.com/work), which lists the projects and clients but not the descriptions. These principles come from the index and the studio's own framing, so open the projects before relying on detail.

1. **Design and film are one department.** The self-description puts design, film and visual arts together, and the index bears it out. Motion is not a deliverable added after the identity.
2. **The product is the material.** [Paper Pro for reMarkable, Mustang GTD for Ford, Gilga Farm for Moncler](https://mvsm.com/work) sit beside [Images 2.0 for OpenAI and Blueprint AI for Squarespace](https://mvsm.com/work): physical objects and software given the same material treatment. Useful when a product has no photogenic object — it can still be given one.
3. **A brand can hold a house language across very different clients.** A single studio voice runs from a car to a note-taking tablet to a trading app. The house language lives in the treatment, not in a palette.

### PORTO ROCHA — [portorocha.com](https://www.portorocha.com/)

Systems built for institutions and consumer brands at the same bar, with a stated position about when to be loud.

1. **Know when to be loud and when to hush.** The studio's own characterisation of its method. The most useful single sentence for a studio that has to decide how much expression a surface can carry — and it implies both, not one.
2. **Derive the system from something real, not from taste.** [MASP](https://www.portorocha.com/masp): the wordmark takes "block-like, rectilinear forms to align with the brutalist style of Lina Bo Bardi's building", and the motion uses "scale contrasts and fluid movement" from her glass easel display system. The building generates the system, which is why it cannot be copied onto another client.
3. **A symbol can be an argument about an institution.** Same project: the two buildings abstracted into one mark, "one rendered in red, one in black", as "a symbol for the new era of the institution".
4. **Complexity in the audience, not simplification.** Its Tinder work is described as "embracing the contradictions of modern dating rather than reducing its audience to a single, simplified persona". The argument against the single invented persona.
5. **Custom type where the system has to be its own.** MASP ships a custom face (FT Aktual MASP, with Formula Type). When a brand's whole argument is distinctiveness, a licensed face is a decision to look at, not a default.

## Using these on a job

- Cite the **principle** and link the source on the board. Never paste the work.
- Three territories in one narrow band of restraint is a **timidity** finding. At least one territory reaches, drawing on the art practice above, and still answers the positioning.
- **The bar is bold and systemic at once.** Bold without a system is decoration and will not survive being handed over. Systemic without boldness is the in-house work the client already has and is hiring the studio to escape.
