# Changelog

## 0.3.7 (26 Sep 2026)

The scope becomes auditable promise by promise, and checking gets an end condition. Every item came out of Cypress, the studio's first job, and most of them out of one failure: the final gate was approved on a pack that did not disclose that a bought deliverable did not exist at all.

- **`studio.py scope-audit`, and `check final` refuses to raise without it.** It generates `gates/scope-audit.md` from `02-scope.md`'s deliverables table, **one line per promise and never one per deliverable**: every format in *Delivered as*, every subject in *Includes*. Cypress's ten rows are 75 promises, 20 formats and 55 subjects. The unit is the promise because **four of the five scope gaps on that job sat inside a row a row-level check passes** - twelve SVG masters existed without the PNG set, the mark PDF or the Design System artifact; `voice.md` existed without its boilerplate; fourteen slides and a correct PDF existed without the `.pptx`; and a twelve-section guidelines microsite existed, internally consistent and complete against `system.md`, with voice missing from it entirely. **Delivery fills it at the end of the making stage and the Producer cross-reads it.** Four closed states, each with evidence the tool checks: `shipped` needs a path that resolves or an `https://` artifact URL, `absent-by-decision` needs a gate that carries a recorded decision, `short` and `client-blocked` need a note. No fifth state and no free text. Regenerating carries answers over by key, so a promise the scope has just gained arrives `unstated` rather than as an absence nobody sees, and an audit older than `02-scope.md` reopens itself. `absent-by-decision` is the state that separates a decision from a gap - Cypress's imagery direction, parked by Tim at the direction gate - and it is safe to offer only because the tool can check it against Tim's own recorded words.
- **The gate pack's `## Scope` section is generated from the audit**, not written. The template had no section that had to be filled from `02-scope.md`, so nothing in the document prompted the check. A `short` row cannot be softened into a risk line, because studio.py writes it.
- **`studio.py cross-read <gate> --by <role> --of <role>`, and no gate but `brief` raises without one.** One role checks another's completeness claim against the commissioning document, never its own. Three roles made the same sourcing mistake on Cypress in one day - the Producer's gate pack written from what roles reported, the Strategist's README from the scope rather than from the file, the Builder's microsite from `system.md` rather than from the scope - and not one caught it in their own work. The Strategist weighted this as the only one of its four proposed rules that matters, on the grounds that the other three ask a role to catch its own blind spot, which is exactly what failed three times. `brief` is exempt: at intake the Producer is the only role in the job and intake validation already holds that gate. The audit carries the same rule as data, and the final gate fails when `filled-by` and `cross-read-by` match.
- **Name the question, then name the source that governs it**, as operating rule 11. *What must exist* is the commissioning document, *what does exist* is the artefact, *what this claim means* is the owner. **Completeness and content need different sources, and anything asserting both needs both** - which is where all three Cypress failures sat, because a gate pack, a stage README and a guidelines microsite each say *this is complete* and *this says X* in one breath and each author verified one half. And where a document describes a set of files, values or strings, generate it from them and fail the build: three documents turned into generators on that job each found a real defect on its first run, of three different classes, and none had been found by a person reading the same file.
- **A verification budget per track, and a stopping rule.** `studio.py verify-pass <key> --found <n>` records each full checking pass; `studio.py verify-close <key> --call "<why>"` is the Producer's explicit call, logged in `90-decisions.md` under a name. **Checking closes only on a pass that found nothing that would ship wrong**, never because patience ran out, and a pass beyond the track's budget (sprint 3, programme 4, the rest 2) needs `--beyond "<reason>"`, which is also logged. `set-active <folder> no` refuses while a verification has passes and no closing call, with `--force` for a job being stopped rather than delivered. Cypress ran four passes at handover with no rule saying when to stop, which is why the close-out had no stopping rule to report.
- **The vault lint is a gate condition, not only a write hook.** `studio.py check <gate>` now runs it over the whole job at every gate and reports literal colours as readiness problems. The `PostToolUse` hook only fires where the hook is in the loop, and **a job driven from the Claude app through the device shell writes every file with no hook running at all**, so on those jobs the lint had never run by the time a gate was raised.
- **Lint defect fixed: SVG editor chrome is no longer a literal colour.** Inkscape and Sodipodi write the canvas colour, the page border and the desk colour into `<sodipodi:namedview>` whether anybody asked or not; none of it renders. Cypress's `tessel-wordmark-v2.svg` failed the lint on exactly that, and a check that fires on an editor's own chrome is a check everyone learns to ignore - which mattered more once the lint became blocking. The skip is narrow: those two namespaces, inside their own elements only, so a literal fill on a path either side is still reported.
- **A negation is still a claim**, in the copy standards, the Copywriter and the Strategist's claim ceilings table. "Not retained beyond the day" asserts deletion exactly as much as "kept for seven years" asserts keeping. The test is mechanical - turn the negation into its positive and ask whether the client could stand behind it - and the scope distinction makes it checkable: a negation about *what we claim* needs no evidence, a negation about *what the software does* is a claim. Every ceilings row carries a Never/Permitted pairing, because a table whose rows all guard positive forms lets every negation through. **Apply the test hardest where the register is warmest**: the same assertion appeared twice on Cypress and the version in the buyer's own vocabulary survived two further passes purely because it sounded like something a real operations director would say. And **which shape a ceiling takes depends on who handles the copy next**, because a stranger's editor deletes negations first.
- **Timidity has a failing test.** `studio-standards/scripts/treatment-check.py` reads three territory boards' own values and compares them on seven treatment dimensions - ground, ink, chroma, radius, gradient, shadow, texture. **Agreement on five of seven is a finding the Creative Lead must argue on the board.** It exists as a script because the author's own claim that a reach is load-bearing is the least reliable evidence available: Cypress's three boards diverged completely on subject, agreed on **seven of seven** treatments, passed the shared-logo rule, failed the reach rule, and nobody noticed until after handover.
- **The Strategist names what each register constraint does not forbid.** "Do not dramatise the break" forbids drama; it does not forbid saturation, scale, depth, density or colour. Cypress ended up restrained because restraint was right about the register and then went further than the register required with nothing to stop it.
- **A `## Transplant` line in the direction gate pack**, and a gate must rule on a declared departure **even in a territory it does not choose**. A board that loses can still be right about one thing, and if nobody asks at the gate the answer is lost with the board. T1's declared vermilion got `n/a`, so the later eval had to re-raise red from scratch.
- **`blenderkit.py` no longer claims to build a scene, because it never did.** It sets the view transform, engine, samples and resolution, moves the camera and renders; it creates no geometry, no materials, no lights and no camera. Run as documented against a factory startup file it renders **Blender's default cube**, correctly exposed and in the brand's colours, which looks like a working render and answers nothing. The skill now shows both ways to supply a scene, prefers a scene script over a `.blend` because a `.blend` is not a record of how the scene was made, and states the rule the livery test proved: whatever the answer depends on has to be in the scene script and stated, or the render is an illustration rather than evidence.
- **Verify by a property of the thing, not by its label**, and **a guard you have not tried against what it must catch is not a guard**, both into Delivery. The first is Cypress's strongest invariant, adopted upstream: an inlined SVG is self-identifying by geometry, and a filename survives being wrong. Its limit is named rather than papered over. The second came from a print-sheet guard that swallowed the ghost master fed in to prove it worked.
- **`studio.py log`**, so the Producer appends decisions through the tool and never types a time again. Every hand-written entry in Cypress's decisions log drifted ahead of the clock, and the last five crossed a midnight that had not happened. The machine already stamps UTC+8 from the Mac mini's own clock; the typist was the only unreliable part. Earlier entries are left as written, because a decisions log that edits its own history is not evidence.
- 199 tests, including one per new check that fails on the Cypress case it was built from.

### Carried forward, not in 0.3.7

Held deliberately rather than dropped, and each is in Cypress's `95-close/fixes.md` with its cost:

- **Nothing makes a stale read visible.** A role read `system.md` nine minutes before its owner revised it, and again five minutes before, the second time nearly publishing a mark minimum the system calls a real defect. Rule 11's generate-from-the-artefact clause removes the class where a document can be generated; the case where a role must simply read another's file still has no guard.
- **A rationalisation generalised into a rule is worse than no rule, because it gets applied.** The withdrawn "1.6px of ground" floor was arithmetic describing one boundary, written up as the principle the boundary came from, and it produced three contradictions in one file. Needs a standard about how a measured number is recorded.
- **Two of the six reach proposals**: give the reach itself a failing test by building the stripped twin and rendering both before the gate, and wire the reach to the expression budget so one expressive cover cannot satisfy the rule on a job briefed bold throughout.
- **Eight of the nine `blender-3d` defects.** `verify` only reads the sidecar rather than re-reading the render; the sidecar omits lens and sensor width, so an angular judgement cannot be reproduced; the Blender version is read from an env var as unknown while `bpy.app.version_string` is available; the draft preset cannot answer a distance question the skill says it can.
- **The `.pptx` build is not byte-reproducible** (zip member ordering and OOXML timestamps), so a repackage must rebuild rather than reuse or it ships two decks from different binaries under names asserting they are identical.
- **`art-sources.md` part two may be in the wrong file.** Almost everything useful on Cypress came from part one, the art practice; the studio principles landed on the Producer and the Identity designer rather than on a board.

## 0.3.6 (25 Sep 2026)

Expressive range. The studio had rules against being generic and none against being timid.

- **The reach rule.** At least one of the three territories draws explicitly on art practice — depth, colour, texture, atypical shape and form — and still answers the positioning. **Three territories inside one narrow band of restraint is a Major finding, and it is called timidity.** It lands on the Creative Lead and, from 0.4.0, on the panel's Creative lens. Argued restraint is a strategy; unargued restraint is a default wearing a strategy's clothes.
- **`studio-standards/references/art-sources.md`.** Art practice as principles to take rather than looks to copy — Constructivism, Op art, Albers, Fauvism, Delaunay, arte povera, Bauhaus weaving, riso and overprint, Suprematism, Memphis, concrete poetry, brutalism — and five studio benchmarks studied on 25 Sep 2026: DIA, Studio Dumbar, Hey Studio, ManvsMachine and PORTO ROCHA. Each carries three to five principles tied to a specific linked project, with the quotes those projects actually publish. ManvsMachine's case pages would not open, and the file says so rather than inventing detail. Boards cite the principle and link the source; no project is imitated, no studio is named in a generation prompt, and no territory is pitched as "like Studio X".
- **The expressive vault layer**, optional and beside the functional one: `expressive.palette`, `.texture`, `.shape`, `.gradient`, `.depth`. A job whose expression budget keeps every surface calm has nothing to put in it, so an unset slot validates; a filled one is type-checked like any other. **A new lint rule: texture never sits behind body text**, detected on the selector, so a hero or a cover is untouched and a `p`, `td` or `.body` is reported. Every contrast rule is unchanged.
- **The expression budget** in the brief, the intake checklist and the Builder: where expression lives (covers, heroes, campaigns, packaging, environments) and where it does not (forms, tables, data, body copy). Default dial, adjusted per job: start-ups bold throughout, corporates bold in brand moments and calm in working surfaces. The line is never drawn through accessibility.
- 159 tests.

## 0.3.5 (25 Sep 2026)

3D gets a reason before it gets a renderer, and the effect check stops passing quietly.

- **The 3D decision rule** in `studio-standards`. A job may use 3D for a physical touchpoint, a spatial territory idea argued on the board, or explanation that needs space. Anything else is a declared departure, because "it would look good in 3D" is not an argument and abstract 3D blobs on gradients are already on the anti-generic list. It runs through the job rather than sitting in a document: the Strategist writes a touchpoint inventory in `10-strategy/touchpoints.md`, every territory board carries a **Dimension** section naming its answer or declaring a departure, the direction gate pack states the plan and Tim approves it, the Identity designer runs Blender mockups for every physical touchpoint at real viewing distances, and the Builder and Art Director build only what was approved.
- **`blender-3d` skill and `blenderkit.py`.** Scenes take their material colours from `vault/build/blender.json` and the view transform is forced to Standard, because Blender's default desaturates every brand colour and nobody can say why the renders drifted. Draft and final presets, headless from the command line, `--distances` so an object is judged at the distance it is actually seen from. Every render writes a sidecar recording the scene, preset, samples, view transform, the vault build it drew from and the command, because a render nobody can reproduce is a screenshot. `verify` reads that sidecar back and refuses a render made under the wrong view transform.
- **"Effect not verified" is now visible.** Where the refinement effect check has no SVG renderer it used to return nothing, which reads as a pass. It now raises a warning that never blocks the gate and is written into the pack above the risks, saying plainly that a person is named and that the pass was **not** checked for having changed anything.
- 125 tests.

## 0.3.4 (25 Sep 2026)

Mandatory intake validation, before a word of the brief is drafted.

- An eleven-item checklist, each marked `supplied`, `inferred` or `missing`: client and decision-maker, what they are buying, the date and what it is tied to, audience, existing brand, budget band, competitors as the client named them, physical touchpoints, platform and who maintains it, confidentiality, success measure. An inference is confirmed with Tim, never promoted to a fact by being written into the brief.
- At least three questions to Tim in one round of three or four, through `AskUserQuestion`, ordered by impact on track, scope, price and date, recommended option first, confirming the inferred rows rather than re-asking the supplied ones. A second round only for gaps that still block the track, the price or the date. Plain numbered questions where `AskUserQuestion` is unavailable.
- Items only the client can answer go to a drafted client email and into the brief's open questions, rather than being put to Tim.
- `studio.py init` writes `00-intake/validation.md` from a template, so the file exists from the first minute and is filled as intake happens. `studio.py check brief` refuses to raise without it, with unfilled prompts in it, or with fewer than three answered questions, and placeholders, "unanswered" and "TBC" do not count as answers.
- Competitors stay client-named in the checklist, the template and the skill, matching 0.3.1.
- 105 tests.

## 0.3.3 (25 Sep 2026)

Everything in this release came out of the studio's first job.

- `builder`: owns `50-applications/` and `50-make/`. Tokens only, never literal values; renders at phone and desktop width and judges the screenshots rather than the markup; never changes the identity to make a build easier, and never puts a mark or name into a deliverable the job's record says is unresolved.
- `delivery`: owns `99-handover/` and `99-return/`. Export matrix, asset index, tokens package and licence notes, packaged against `02-scope.md` line by line rather than against the folder. Opens every file it ships, never fixes a defect quietly, and ships nothing the record shows is unresolved.
- `studio-close` skill: turns a finished job into an intervention log, a fix list and eval cases drawn from `90-decisions.md` and Tim's gate replies. It counts Tim's decisions, Tim's corrections and role-to-role catches separately, because they mean different things, and it forbids writing a case for something that did not happen.
- **The refinement effect check.** A person-attributed version that renders identically to the one before it is now refused at the system gate. Found on Cypress: a hand file saved from a real editor, logged in good faith, with the change absent. Attribution without effect is not evidence. Renders through whichever of rsvg-convert, inkscape or cairosvg is present and compares decoded pixels with a standard-library PNG decoder; on a machine with no renderer it checks attribution and stays silent rather than blocking work it cannot assess.
- The Producer labels every figure an agent reports as reported until it has checked it. On the first job a contrast ratio, a small-size type table and a customer statistic all travelled upward unverified, and one reached Tim as fact.
- 96 tests.

## 0.3.0 (25 Sep 2026)

The four creative roles. A brand sprint now has someone to do every stage of it.

- `strategist`: owns `10-strategy/`. Tests the brief's working proposition against a rival reading rather than adopting it, applies the competitor-swap test to its own positioning, and hands the Creative Lead the constraints the territories have to answer. Evidence is sourced, attributed or flagged; never manufactured.
- `creative-lead`: owns `20-territories/`. Three different bets, each with a board that names its idea, what it gives up, its declared departures from the anti-generic list, its references and how it would fail. Boards are rendered and read on a phone before review. Literal colour values are allowed in territory boards, and only there, because the vault does not exist until the identity stage.
- `copywriter`: owns `voice.md`, `naming.md` and every `*-copy.md`, wherever they sit, the one role that owns files rather than a folder. Voice before lines, the competitor-swap test on every line, copy judged in place at real length, and name screening recorded.
- `identity-designer`: owns `30-identity/` and `vault/`. Marks tested at 16px, 32px and 512px, in one colour, reversed and on photography before discussion. Builds and validates the vault, and hands `swatches.html` to the Producer rather than publishing it. No generated image is ever a final mark: the agent constructs and prepares the master, and the pass that makes it final is made by a named human. `30-identity/refinement-log.md` records it, and `studio.py check system` will not pass without an entry naming a person. `originality.md` covers marks only; names are screened by the Copywriter, on every job.
- Vault lint and hook skip `20-territories/` and `30-identity/exploration/`. Both are pre-vault by design, and a lint that cries wolf there is one everyone learns to ignore.
- Producer roster names the agent to launch for each role, and how to brief one. The panel and the control room move to 0.4.0.
- 63 tests.

## 0.2.0 (25 Sep 2026)

The Producer, the standards and the brand vault. The studio stands on its own; the engagement studio is one of its requesters.

- Producer operating model: five tracks (sprint, launch-kit, product-ui, programme, commission), roster and file ownership, gates, client feedback triage, commissions, where jobs live, output catalogue.

- Stage skills: `studio-intake`, `studio-gate`, `studio-status`, `commission`. Named so they do not trigger on engagement studio work.
- Templates: creative brief, scope of work (draft statement of work with ownership, licensing and AI-use terms), phone-readable gate pack, commission brief.
- `studio-standards`: operating rules, craft and copy standards, references and originality, file conventions, and the anti-generic list.
- Brand vault: W3C design token template, `vault.py` validate (structure, values, aliases, cycles, WCAG AA on body text), build (CSS custom properties, flat JSON, linear colours for Blender, a tokens page), contrast matrix and lint. A hook reports literal colours written into job files.
- Art Director loads the standards and the vault, and works under external brands on commissions.
- 37 tests.
